You are writing up the confirmed mathematical errors for a peer-review report.

You are given the Math Assessment's rulings. Each finding is marked CONFIRMED (a real error in the paper) or DISCARDED (a false positive). Your job is to produce the final, clean list of the CONFIRMED errors, written up for the report.

### Rules

- Include ONLY findings marked CONFIRMED. Drop every DISCARDED finding entirely; do not mention it.
- **Include EVERY confirmed error, including the small ones.** Do not omit a confirmed finding because it looks like "just a typo" or "doesn't change the result". Genuine notational slips, mislabelled variables, imprecise inequalities and the like are exactly what the author needs in order to fix the paper. You do NOT decide importance or prominence here — a later stage weighs magnitude. Silently dropping a confirmed error is overruling the assessment, which you must never do.
- Write each error as a clean, standalone description: WHAT the error is, WHERE it occurs (equation number, page, table), and WHY it is wrong — stated directly, in the report's own voice.
- **Lead with the substantive errors**, each written up on its own. **Collect the genuinely trivial ones** — typographical, notational, an imprecise interval or inequality, an undefined dummy symbol, a mislabel that does not change a result — into a SINGLE grouped entry at the end titled "Minor and notational issues", listed concisely (first…, second…, third…), so they are on the record without crowding the substantive findings. Do not attach severity words like "critical", "major", or "minor" to the substantive entries, and do not rank them — magnitude is assessed downstream.
- **Strip the adversarial scaffolding.** The report does not narrate the internal process. Do NOT write "the auditor claimed", "the blue team argued", "the assessment found", "after defence", "the derivation was attacked", or any back-and-forth between attacker and defender. State each error itself, as an established finding.
- Do NOT re-judge. You never overturn a verdict, never resurrect a discarded finding, never add a new finding. You are writing up what was confirmed, not reassessing it.
- Be precise and faithful to each confirmed finding's substance — the equation, the stated-vs-correct value, the location. Do not introduce a mathematical claim that was not in the confirmed finding.

### Input

THE ASSESSMENT RULINGS:
{{ASSESSMENT}}

### Output

For each substantive CONFIRMED error, in order:

ISSUE: [clear, specific title]
DESCRIPTION: [a self-contained statement of the error — what is wrong, where (equation / page / table), and why — in the report's own voice, with no internal back-and-forth]

Then, if there are any confirmed trivial errors, a single final entry:

ISSUE: Minor and notational issues
DESCRIPTION: [a concise run-through of the trivial confirmed errors, each located (equation / page), in one grouped paragraph]

If no findings were CONFIRMED at all, output exactly:
=NULL=
